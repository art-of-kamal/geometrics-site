Most marketing teams test their AI visibility the same way: they open ChatGPT, type *"What is the best [category] tool?"*, see their brand mentioned once, and assume they are covered.

That single check is misleading for two reasons: **different AI engines pull from completely different sources**, and **their answers don't stay still**.

## The 11% Overlap Problem

A 2026 analysis of 100,000 AI search prompts found only an **11% domain overlap** between the sources ChatGPT cites and the sources Perplexity cites. Even when two buyers ask the exact same question on the same day, the underlying web pages shaping the answer are largely different.

A separate October 2026 comparison study showed a similar gap in how often models cite official brand websites in head-to-head queries:
- **ChatGPT** cited both brands' websites in roughly **77%** of comparison answers.
- **Perplexity** did so in about **50%**.
- **Google AI Overviews** dropped to **37%**.

If your brand is winning on ChatGPT because of strong product pages, you can still be nearly invisible on Perplexity or Google AI Overviews if third-party review sites and industry roundups aren't mentioning you.

## Answers Drift Faster Than Google Rankings

In traditional SEO, moving from position #2 to #7 usually takes a core update or months of competitor work. In AI search, visibility is probabilistic. Research from the *2026 State of AI Search* report found that **only 30% of brands stay consistently visible** across repeated AI sessions.

A single prompt run tells you what happened once. It doesn't tell you your actual share of voice.

## What to Measure Instead

Before spending budget on content rewrites or GEO agencies, get a clean baseline across the engines your buyers actually use:

1. **Test across all major engines** — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews.
2. **Track real buyer prompts** — Discovery (*"best tools for..."*), comparison (*"Brand A vs Brand B"*), and use-case queries.
3. **Audit the cited sources** — Look at which third-party domains each engine trusts in your category, not just whether your own homepage appeared.
